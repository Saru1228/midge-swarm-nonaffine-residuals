\begin{abstract}
Laboratory midge swarms remain cohesive without strong global velocity
alignment, making them useful for asking which components of collective motion
remain after ordinary local geometry is removed. We re-analyzed nineteen
three-dimensional trajectories of \textit{Chironomus riparius} swarms and
defined T1 as a local tangential non-affine activity computed from
focal-neighborhood relative displacements after local affine deformation was
fitted and subtracted. Under a frozen centered-detrending definition and a
two-scale consistency requirement, T1 survived in 14 of 19 observations; under
a more permissive any-scale criterion, it survived in 15 of 19 observations.
A completed full-pipeline pseudo-event calibration found that none of 1000
null replicates reached the observed both-scale count (plus-one empirical
$p\approx0.001$), and local-affine conditioning quality control found
well-conditioned fits. However, a detrending challenge reduced
both-scale support to 11 of 19 under past-only detrending and 13 of 19 without
rolling detrending, so the exact 14 of 19 count should not be read as
preprocessing-invariant. Within the survivor class, 14 of 15 observations
remained stable under nearby scale and temporal-lag perturbations. The most
repeatable phenotype was diffuse tangential activity, supported in 13 of 14
tested observations, whereas near-pre timing, edge/core contrast, and signed
event-type structure were weaker or more heterogeneous. Compact state
variables did not provide stable grouped out-of-sample improvement for first-
or second-moment prediction, and true transition timestamps did not add robust
near-pre excess after state-matched controls. Recent state-path history was
informative in some observations but did not define a universal memory rule.
These results identify T1 as a measurable local residual layer between raw
individual trajectories and low-dimensional swarm state. The contribution is
therefore diagnostic rather than mechanistic: T1 is common under the tested
local affine reduction, but not universal or fully explained by the reductions
examined here.
\end{abstract}
