# Title and Abstract Draft v3

## Working Title

Local Affine Subtraction Reveals Persistent Tangential Non-Affine Activity in
Laboratory Midge Swarms

## Shorter Title Candidate

Local Tangential Non-Affine Activity Persists in Laboratory Midge Swarms After
Local Affine Subtraction

## Residual-Layer Title Candidate

A Measurable Local Residual Layer in Laboratory Midge Swarms After Affine

## Abstract

Laboratory midge swarms remain cohesive without strong global velocity
alignment, making them useful for asking which components of collective motion
remain after ordinary local geometry is removed. We re-analyzed nineteen
three-dimensional trajectories of *Chironomus riparius* swarms and defined T1
as a local tangential non-affine activity computed from focal-neighborhood
relative displacements after local affine deformation was fitted and
subtracted.

Under a frozen centered-detrending definition and a two-scale consistency
requirement, T1 survived in 14 of 19 observations; under a more permissive
any-scale criterion, it survived in 15 of 19 observations. A completed
full-pipeline pseudo-event calibration found that none of 1000 null replicates
reached the observed both-scale count (plus-one empirical p approximately
0.001), and local-affine conditioning quality control found well-conditioned
fits. However, a detrending challenge reduced both-scale support to 11 of 19
under past-only detrending and 13 of 19 without rolling detrending, so the exact
14 of 19 count should not be read as preprocessing-invariant. Within the
survivor class, 14 of 15 observations remained stable under nearby scale and
temporal-lag perturbations. The most repeatable phenotype was diffuse
tangential activity, supported in 13 of 14 tested observations, whereas
near-pre timing, edge/core contrast, and signed event-type structure were
weaker or more heterogeneous.

Compact state variables did not provide stable grouped out-of-sample
improvement for first- or second-moment prediction, and true transition
timestamps did not add robust near-pre excess after state-matched controls.
Recent state-path history was informative in some observations but did not
define a universal memory rule. These results identify T1 as a measurable
local residual layer between raw individual trajectories and low-dimensional
swarm state. The contribution is therefore diagnostic rather than mechanistic:
T1 is common under the tested local affine reduction, but not universal or fully
explained by the reductions examined here.

## Keywords

midge swarms; non-affine motion; local affine subtraction; collective motion;
compact state variables; observation heterogeneity

## Current Framing Decision

The title and abstract should frame the paper as a bounded phenomenon and
reduction-boundary study. The main contribution is not a universal mechanism.
It is the identification of a measurable local residual layer after ordinary
local affine geometry has been removed, with explicit calibration by a
full-pipeline pseudo-event null and with observation-level boundaries for
several simple explanatory reductions.
